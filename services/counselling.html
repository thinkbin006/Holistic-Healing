About this practice

It can be useful to set aside time to put everyday concerns into words. Here, Counselling describes supportive wellbeing conversations, not a clinical service. You might want to reflect on a decision, explore personal priorities or think about how to express a need in everyday life.

What to expect

Bring a concern you would like to discuss with Ruchi, even if it is not yet neatly defined. The focus is on your perspective, the questions that matter to you and the practical choices you can consider. You decide what you feel ready to share.

A conversation might start with identifying what feels most important now. From there, you could reflect on existing sources of support or a small next step that feels manageable. The intention is to make room for thought, not to provide a diagnosis or tell you how your life should unfold.

Clinical qualifications and scope have not been confirmed for this offering. It is not psychotherapy, mental-health assessment or treatment, crisis support, or medical care. Where those forms of help are needed, contact an appropriately qualified professional rather than relying on a wellbeing conversation.

Preparing for your session

You can bring a short question or a few optional notes. There is no need to send diagnoses, trauma histories or detailed personal information with an initial enquiry.

Before sharing sensitive matters, ask Ruchi about the boundaries of the conversation and how information is handled. Discuss accessibility or seating needs, too. Ask Ruchi about the session format, duration and fee before arranging a session.
