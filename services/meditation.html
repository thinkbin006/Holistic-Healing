About this practice

Meditation does not require a perfectly quiet mind. It can begin with noticing what is here and gently returning your attention when it wanders. Guided Meditation may interest you if you are new to the practice or would like to explore it with someone explaining the steps.

What to expect

Ruchi's approach includes guided attention through breath awareness, visualisation and mindfulness practices. Ask which approach she suggests and let her know what feels comfortable. You do not need to use every technique or imagine vivid pictures for the practice to be worth exploring.

A simple intention might be to notice a natural breath, a sound or your contact with the chair. Thoughts may continue throughout. Instead of measuring success by how calm you feel, consider whether you can notice your experience without demanding that it be different.

Stillness and inward attention feel different for different people. If focusing on breathing or closing your eyes feels unsettling, say so and ask about another focus. You can keep your eyes open, change position or stop. No particular emotional state or mental-health outcome is promised.

Preparing for your session

Wear comfortable clothing and discuss seating that suits your body. If you are choosing a space, a reasonably quiet spot and a chair are enough; a cushion, incense and floor sitting are not requirements.

Arrive with curiosity rather than a target to meet. Mention preferences without feeling obliged to disclose a personal history. Ask Ruchi about the session format, duration and fee before arranging a session.

Before you begin

A little more clarity.

What if my mind keeps wandering?+

Wandering thoughts do not mean you are failing. The practice can involve noticing that attention has moved and gently returning to a chosen focus.

Do I have to sit on the floor or close my eyes?+

No. Discuss comfortable seating and keeping your eyes open with Ruchi. You can ask for a different focus or pause if a practice feels uncomfortable.
